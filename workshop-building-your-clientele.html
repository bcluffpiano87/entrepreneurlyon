Framework 2 — Your Referral Email

Source: Aaron Ross, Predictable Revenue

Ross' referral email is deliberately short — under 100 words, no pitch, no links, no attachments. You're asking to be pointed to the right person, not asking for a meeting. This format generated a 9–15% response rate at Salesforce.

Subject: Can you point me in the right direction?

[First name],

I'm sorry to trouble you. Would you be so kind as to tell me who is responsible for _____________ at [Company] and how I might get in touch with them?

Thank you,
[Your name]

Framework 3 — Your Phone Script

Source: Jeb Blount, Fanatical Prospecting

Blount's 5-step framework: (1) Use their first name, (2) State your name and company, (3) Tell them why you're calling — "The reason I'm calling is…", (4) Bridge with a "because" — a trigger event, stat, or referral, (5) Ask for a specific day and time — then SHUT UP. Silence is your friend.

Framework 4 — Your Networking Question

Source: Bob Burg, Endless Referrals

At any networking event, ask these 4 "feel-good questions" first. Let them talk for 15–20 minutes. Do not pitch. Then ask the ONE key question that sets you apart from everyone else at the event.

The 4 Feel-Good Questions

  1. "What company are you with?"
  2. "What do you do there?"
  3. "How long have you been doing that?"
  4. "What do you like best about what you do?"

The Key Question

"I always enjoy connecting good people with other good people. Tell me — how would I know if someone I'm speaking with would be a good prospect for you?"

Your Answer — The Critical Fill-In

When they ask you the same question back (and they will), what do you say?

Your answer to "How would I know if someone is a good prospect for YOU?"Write a specific, visual, memorable answer. NOT "anyone who needs my product." Think: the copier salesman who said "Look for offices with overflowing wastebaskets next to their copiers." What's YOUR version? What visible sign tells you someone needs what you sell?
Practice saying this out loud 3 times before the event. It must feel natural, not rehearsed. If it sounds like a pitch, rewrite it. If a stranger can picture it, you've got it right.

Framework 5 — Your Offer

Source: Alex Hormozi, $100M Offers

Hormozi's Value Equation: Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood) ÷ (Time Delay × Effort). Increase the top two, decrease the bottom two. Pros attack the bottom — make it immediate and effortless.

Part A: Your Value Equation

Lever Question Your Answer
The emotional end-state: peace of mind, pride, relief, confidence. Be specific.
Dream Outcome ↑ What does your client FEEL when you've done your job perfectly?
A case study, demo, guarantee, or track record. Your strongest proof.
Perceived Likelihood ↑ Why should they believe it will work?
Not the final result — the FIRST tangible sign. "Day 1" beats "3 months."
Time Delay ↓ How fast do they see the first result?
Less is better. "Nothing — we do it for them" is the ideal answer.
Effort & Sacrifice ↓ What do they have to DO or GIVE UP?

Framework 6 — Your 90-Day Rocks

Source: Gino Wickman, Traction / EOS

Framework 7 — Your One-Page Strategic Plan

Source: Verne Harnish, Scaling Up

Harnish says every person in your company must be able to articulate who you serve and why you're different. This one-page plan forces clarity.

Field Your Answer
Your one-liner In one sentence: what do you do, for whom, and why is it different? Test: could a stranger repeat this after hearing it once?
Your ideal client Be specific. Industry, size, location, pain. Not "any business" — the ONE type you serve best.
Your brand promise What do you guarantee your client will experience? Not a feature — a result.
Your 3-year target Where do you want to be in 3 years? Revenue, client count, team size. One sentence.
Your 1-year target Where do you want to be in 12 months? Same format.
Your #1 priority this quarter The single most important thing. If you could only accomplish ONE thing in the next 90 days, what is it?
Harnish: "Growth sucks cash." Before you grow, know your Cash Conversion Cycle — how long between spending a euro and getting it back. If growth starves your cash, you'll fail with a full pipeline.
